Share the trips you have taken. Find someone to take the next one with.

You post photographs and a few honest lines from where you have been. People looking for a companion read that before they decide to spend ten days with you.

Coming to the App Store iPhone, free.

Sharing is the way in

A trip is a few photographs, where and when, and two or three sentences that are true. Not a review, not a travelogue. That is your travel résumé, and it says more about how you travel than any profile field could.

Finding company is the way out

When you want someone along, post the trip: the route, the dates, how many people it is for. Posts sort by departure date, nothing else. No score decides who you see.

How it works

  1. 1

    Share a trip you have taken

    Pick the photographs, name the place, write a few lines. Location and device details are removed from every photograph before it leaves your phone.

  2. 2

    Post the next one

    A route of stops, the dates with a little flexibility, and how many people the trip is for. You decide who may apply.

  3. 3

    Apply by writing

    There is no swipe to join. Applying means writing to the organiser, at least a few sentences. Saving a post keeps it on a private list and tells nobody.

  4. 4

    One conversation per trip

    Nobody can message you until you have said yes. Once you have, the trip has one conversation, and everyone in the party is in it.

Safe enough to actually use

Meeting a stranger for ten days is a bigger decision than a dinner. The rules are built for that, and they are not optional.

  • Identity checked by a person. Posting a trip requires it. The document is deleted after the check; only the mark stays.
  • Women only, if you say so. A post can be women only, men only or same gender only, and people who do not match never see it.
  • No messaging before acceptance. There is no way to write to someone who has not accepted you. None.
  • Report and block from anywhere. Reports are acted on within 24 hours. Some categories hide the content at once.
  • Your photographs stay yours. Metadata is stripped on the phone. You choose who sees each trip: everyone, verified people, or people you have travelled with.
  • Nothing is tracked. No advertising, no analytics, nothing sold. Sign in with Apple or an email code; there is no password and no phone number.

Questions

Is this a dating app?
No. Nothing in it works like one. Saving a post tells nobody, applying means writing, and a trip is a party of people rather than a pair. People do meet, but that is what travel does.
What does it cost?
Nothing. There are no paid features and no advertising.
Where does it work?
Anywhere. The app is in English and Traditional Chinese, and a trip can be in any country, or several. It starts with people in Taiwan, because that is where it was made.
Why does posting a trip require identity verification?
Because someone is about to decide whether to travel with you. The check is done by a person, the document is deleted afterwards, and only the fact that you were verified is kept.
Do I have to share my own trips?
Not to look around, or to apply. But a blank profile almost never gets accepted, and the point of the photographs is that they tell people who you are before any message does.
When?
Soon. The app is finished and being tested. Write to hello@cubip.com if you want to hear when it is out.
